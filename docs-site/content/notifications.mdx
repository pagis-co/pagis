A Notification tells you that something needs you: an approval, a
question, a failed Run or a missed Call. Pagis sends it as a Web Push to
each browser and app where you turn on Notifications, also when no Pagis
window is open. Each of these browsers and apps holds a Push Subscription.
[ADR-0030](https://github.com/pagis-co/pagis/blob/main/docs/adr/0030-a-notification-is-a-web-push-to-each-push-subscription.md)
holds the decision.

Open **Settings → Notifications** in the Product App. The page shows the
state of this browser, and it lists every Push Subscription of yours.

## Turn on Notifications in a browser

1. Open Pagis in Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari, on a computer or on an
   Android phone.
2. Open **Settings → Notifications** and select **Turn on**.
3. The browser asks if the site can show notifications. Select **Allow**.

The page then shows **On**, and the list shows the browser as "This
browser". Select **Send a test** on its row to get a Notification at once.

A browser gives the Push API only to a secure address: `https://`, or
`http://localhost`. On another `http://` address, the page shows that
notifications are not available.

## Turn on Notifications on an iPhone or an iPad

On iOS and iPadOS 16.4 and later, only a web app on the Home Screen gets
Notifications. A Safari tab does not, and in a Safari tab the page shows
these steps:

1. Open Pagis in Safari.
2. Select Share, then **Add to Home Screen**.
3. Open Pagis from the Home Screen. The web app does not share the cookies
   of Safari, so sign in again in the web app.
4. Open **Settings → Notifications** and select **Turn on**. Then select
   **Allow**.

## Turn on Notifications in the Mobile App

1. In the Mobile App, open **Settings → Notifications** and select
   **Turn on**.
2. The phone asks if Pagis can send notifications. Select **Allow**.

The page then shows **On**, and the list shows the app as "This app". In
a browser, the list names it "Pagis on iPhone", "Pagis on iPad" or "Pagis
on Android".

The app gets each Notification through the Pagis Push Relay, which
forwards it to Apple or Google. The Push Relay, Apple and Google see only
the encrypted Notification. Only the phone holds the key that decrypts
it.

When you select **Don't Allow**, the page shows that the phone blocks
notifications from Pagis. Open the Settings app of the phone, find Pagis,
and turn on its notifications. Then open the page again.

On an iPhone or an iPad, the app decrypts each Notification and shows its
text. When the app cannot decrypt or read a Notification, it shows "Pagis"
and "Something needs you" in place of the text.

On an Android phone, the app also decrypts each Notification and shows
its text. A Notification for an approval, a Run that waits for you or a
Keypad Code delay shows in the channel "Needs you". Each other
Notification shows in the channel "Activity". To change how a channel
alerts you, open the Settings app of the phone and find the notifications
of Pagis. When the app cannot decrypt or read a Notification, it shows
"Pagis" and "Something needs you".

## Open a Notification in the Mobile App

Select a Notification to open its place in Pagis, for example the Thread
of an approval, with the approval card in view. When Pagis is open
already, it goes to that place and does not load again. A Notification that names a place on
another server opens Home.

When you open the Mobile App, it removes each Notification of something
that does not need you now, for example an approval that you answered in
a browser. On an iPhone or an iPad, the number on the app icon shows how
many things need you.

## Allow notifications that the browser blocks

When you select **Block** at the question of the browser, the page shows
that this browser blocks notifications from the site. Pagis cannot ask
again. Allow notifications for the site in the browser, then open the page
again:

- **Chrome and Edge**: select the icon at the left of the address, and set
  **Notifications** to **Allow**.
- **Firefox**: select the icon at the left of the address, and clear the
  blocked permission to send notifications.
- **Safari on a Mac**: open **Safari → Settings → Websites →
  Notifications**, and set the site to **Allow**.
- **A Home Screen web app on iOS**: open the **Settings** app of the phone,
  select **Notifications**, then **Pagis**, and turn on **Allow
  Notifications**.

## Answer an approval from a Notification

Chrome, Edge and Firefox, on a computer and on an Android phone, show two
buttons on a Notification of an approval: **Approve once** and **Deny**. Select one to
answer the approval without opening Pagis. The browser sends the answer
with your sign-in, and the Run continues with your answer.

- **Approve once** lets the Agent do this one action.
- **Deny** refuses the action.

When Pagis does not take the answer, the Notification shows "Pagis did not
take this answer. Open Pagis to see the request." This occurs when another
browser or app answered first, when your sign-in ended, or when Pagis did
not answer in 20 seconds. Select the Notification to open the approval.

In the Mobile App on an iPhone or an iPad, touch and hold a Notification
of an approval to show **Approve once** and **Deny**. When the phone is
locked, it asks for Face ID, Touch ID or the passcode before it sends the
answer. Pagis does not open. When Pagis takes the answer, the Notification
goes away. When Pagis does not take it, the app shows the same text as a
browser. Select that Notification to open Pagis.

In the Mobile App on an Android phone, a Notification of an approval
shows **Approve once** and **Deny**. If you do not see them, expand the
Notification. On Android 12 and later,
when the phone is locked, it asks you to unlock it before it sends the
answer. Pagis does not open. When the phone has no network, the answer
waits for the network. When Pagis takes the answer, the Notification goes
away. When Pagis does not take it, the app shows the same text as a
browser. Select that Notification to open Pagis.

Safari on a Mac and in a Home Screen web app on iOS shows no buttons on a
Notification. Select the Notification to open the approval
card.

**Always allow** is only in the approval card, because the card shows what
the rule covers. Open the approval to make a rule.

## Turn off Notifications

Select **Turn off** in **Settings → Notifications**. The browser ends its
Push Subscription, and Pagis deletes it. In the Mobile App, Pagis deletes
the Push Subscription, and the app deletes its registration with the Push
Relay.

## The list of Push Subscriptions

The list shows each Push Subscription of yours, in each browser and app
where you turned on Notifications. Each row names the browser or the app of
its Session, as **Settings → Sessions** does, or "This browser" for the
browser that you use now. The row shows when Pagis last sent a Notification
to it.

- **Send a test** sends a Notification with the text "Notifications work
  here." The row then shows what the push service answered.
- **Remove** deletes the Push Subscription. That browser or app gets no
  Notification until you turn on Notifications there again.

A Push Subscription belongs to one Session. It also ends when its Session
ends, at sign-out or when you remove the Session in **Settings →
Sessions**, and when the push service of the browser tells Pagis that the
subscription ended.

The list of the server is the truth. When this browser holds a Push
Subscription that the server does not list, the page ends the subscription
in the browser and shows **Off**. This occurs after you sign in again, for
example. Select **Turn on** to get Notifications again. The Mobile App also
shows **Off** after you sign in again, and **Turn on** uses its
registration with the Push Relay again.

## The Client App

The Client App gets no Notification, because Electron gives no push
service. Its **Settings → Notifications** tells you to turn on
Notifications in a browser. The Client App shows the Needs-You Queue on
Home, the count in the sidebar and the sound cues.
